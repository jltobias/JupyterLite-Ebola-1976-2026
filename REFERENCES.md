# References and attributions

Accessed through 25 November 2026. These sources support the README, Jupyter Book, JupyterLite Lab, literature review, map catalog, and StoryMap plan. A citation does not itself grant permission to republish a work; file-specific reuse terms are noted below.

## Foundational publications

**Featured anniversary lectures:** Piot, Peter, and Jean-Jacques Muyembe-Tamfum. “50 years of Ebola research and response.” *The Lancet*, published online 10 October 2026. [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01813-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01813-1); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42858755/). The article contains Piot's “Rudolf Virchow revisited: 50 years of viruses, science, people, and politics” and Muyembe-Tamfum's “Pioneering Ebola research and response in the Democratic Republic of the Congo: lessons from 50 years.” The PDF states © 2026 Elsevier Ltd., all rights reserved. It is a historical perspective, not a map dataset or a reuse license for its figures or portraits.

**Disease primer:** Jacob, Shevin T., Ian Crozier, William A. Fischer II, et al. “Ebola virus disease.” *Nature Reviews Disease Primers* 6 (2020): article 13. Published 20 February 2020. [doi:10.1038/s41572-020-0147-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41572-020-0147-3). A clinical and biological review focused on disease caused by Ebola virus (EBOV). Its figures, including the outbreak map, are linked rather than reproduced; the article page does not grant a Creative Commons reuse license for them.

### Landmark studies and a citation survey

- Pattyn, Stefaan, Guido van der Groen, Wim Jacob, Peter Piot, and Jacques Courteille. “Isolation of Marburg-like virus from a case of haemorrhagic fever in Zaire.” *The Lancet* 1, no. 8011 (1977): 573–574. [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(77)92002-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(77)92002-5); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/65663/). Early laboratory isolation following the 1976 Yambuku outbreak; the historical title uses “Marburg-like” before the virus was distinguished and named.
- Feldmann, Heinz, and Thomas W. Geisbert. “Ebola haemorrhagic fever.” *The Lancet* 377, no. 9768 (2011): 849–862. [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(10)60667-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)60667-8); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21084112/). A clinical and virological review reflecting evidence available in 2011.
- Baize, Sylvain, Delphine Pannetier, Lisa Oestereich, et al. “Emergence of Zaire Ebola Virus Disease in Guinea.” *New England Journal of Medicine* 371, no. 15 (2014): 1418–1425. Published online 16 April 2014. [doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1404505](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1404505); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24738640/). Early virological and epidemiological investigation of the Guinea outbreak.
- Urbanowicz, Richard A., C. Patrick McClure, Anavaj Sakuntabhai, et al. “Human Adaptation of Ebola Virus during the West African Outbreak.” *Cell* 167, no. 4 (2016): 1079–1087.e5. [doi:10.1016/j.cell.2016.10.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.10.013); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27814505/). Laboratory pseudovirus and cell-line findings about glycoprotein variants; increased human-cell infectivity is not, by itself, a measured outbreak transmission rate.
- Goldstein, Tracey, Simon J. Anthony, Aiah Gbakima, et al. “The discovery of Bombali virus adds further support for bats as hosts of ebolaviruses.” *Nature Microbiology* 3 (2018): 1084–1089. [doi:10.1038/s41564-018-0227-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-018-0227-2); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30150734/). A bat-virus discovery, not a mapped human Ebola outbreak or proof that this virus causes human disease.
- Kawuki, Joseph, Taha Hussein Musa, Shireen Salome Papabathini, Upama Ghimire, Nathan Obore, and Xiaojin Yu. “The 100 Top-cited Studies on Ebola: A Bibliometric Analysis.” *Electronic Journal of General Medicine* 18, no. 2 (2021): em276. [doi:10.29333/ejgm/9694](https://doi.org/10.29333/ejgm/9694); [publisher page](https://www.ejgm.co.uk/article/the-100-top-cited-studies-on-ebola-a-bibliometric-analysis-9694). Citation counts come from a Web of Science search conducted in January 2020; the ranking is a historical snapshot, not a current impact measure or a map-data source. The article is CC BY, as stated on its publisher page.

**Historical-date note:** The second lecture says Kikwit re-emerged in 1998, but its own cited [Khan et al. study](https://doi.org/10.1086/514306) and the [CDC outbreak chronology](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/outbreaks/) identify the Kikwit outbreak as 1995. The StoryMap uses 1995.
