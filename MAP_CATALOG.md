## Third-party maps in the cross-border brief

[Palmer et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.19088/MHRN.2026.001) is valuable for 2026 borderland context, and its text is CC BY 4.0. Three maps in the supplied PDF are explicitly marked “Reproduced with permission” from other creators. That permission to the brief does not establish a Creative Commons license for reuse in this project, so these figures are **excluded from the reusable-visual table**:

- **Figure 1, p. 10:** DRC health zones and South Sudan counties at risk, credited to IMPACT Initiatives (2026). The brief cites an [August 2026 REACH regional overview](https://repository.impact-initiatives.org/document/repository/2c9caf35/REACH_COD_Map_RegionalOverviewMobility_20260818_A4_3pages.pdf) as underlying context; verify that original map's own terms before any reuse.
- **Figure 2, p. 11:** South Sudan high-risk counties, travel corridors, and partners, credited to WHO (2026). Its geographic risk and operational categories are dated, not confirmed case locations.
- **Figure 3, p. 19:** Informal crossings and mobility corridors, credited to IOM Displacement Tracking Matrix South Sudan. Do not publish precise informal-route geometry from this scan without source permission and a review of the safety and context of those locations.

## Suggested credit pattern

For any reused map, place a visible credit close to the visual: **“Map: [creator], [title and map date] ([file link]), [license link]; outbreak data: [named authority and report date]; adapted by [project author], [changes].”** Retain credits printed inside an ERCC map. For CC BY-SA adaptations, follow the same-license requirement. For a CDC public-domain map, still identify CDC, the date, and the source so readers can trace the data. [Wikimedia reuse guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia/en)

## Rebuildable maps and gaps

- The [1976 field reports](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2395561/) and [Zaire report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2395567/) identify early locations. Rebuild a clean locator from those reports and [Natural Earth public-domain boundaries](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/), labeling coordinates as approximate if the publication only gives a named place.
- The [Mylne et al. collection](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.1168886) is the best identified starting point for pre-2014 GIS layers. It is a historical compilation, not a complete 1976–2026 dataset. Check each dataset-item license and citation before committing data files.
- For later outbreaks, assemble **separate dated layers** from outbreak-specific WHO and CDC reports rather than deriving 2026 locations from an older overview map. A health-zone polygon or map symbol should never be presented as the exact residence of a patient.
- No 3D outbreak map is endorsed as an epidemiologic source. If a 3D view is built, treat elevation as geographic context, identify its terrain source and date, and give the same case-data attribution as the corresponding 2D layer.
