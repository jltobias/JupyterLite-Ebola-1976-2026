**Explore 50 years of Ebola history through place and time.** This repository publishes a Map Tour and timeline, a [Jupyter Book](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026/book/), and a [browser-based JupyterLite Lab](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026/lab/lab/index.html). It begins with the 1976 outbreaks in present-day South Sudan and the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and extends through the ongoing 2026 Bundibugyo virus disease outbreak. The 2026 chapter must be updated as official reports change. [WHO disease overview](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ebola-disease) · [CDC outbreak history](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/outbreaks/) · [WHO situation report, 20 November 2026](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON630)

**Featured 50-year perspective:** Peter Piot and Jean-Jacques Muyembe-Tamfum, [“50 years of Ebola research and response”](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01813-1), *The Lancet* (2026). Their paired lectures connect discovery and field investigation in 1976 with community-led response, research during outbreaks, and the institutions needed for future preparedness. See the [literature review](LITERATURE_REVIEW.md) and [full reference](REFERENCES.md#foundational-publications).

**Landmark research:** The [literature review](LITERATURE_REVIEW.md#landmark-papers-across-the-five-decades) connects the 1977 virus-isolation report, 2011 clinical review, 2014 Guinea and genomic-surveillance studies, 2016 viral-adaptation experiment, and 2018 Bombali virus discovery. [Full publication citations](REFERENCES.md#landmark-studies-and-a-citation-survey) include a 2021 bibliometric survey whose citation ranking reflects a January 2020 search.

## Story at a glance

The live StoryMap keeps **place and time visible at every stop**: a persistent locator map, an active year or date range, and a chapter progress marker. Each Map Tour stop pairs a place with a dated event and source-linked evidence or historical visuals. The tour is a curated introduction, not a complete outbreak registry. Its narrative and expansion plan are in the [StoryMap notes](STORYMAP_PLAN.md).

**Terminology matters.** “Ebola disease” is the broad category; Ebola virus, Sudan virus, and Bundibugyo virus are distinct causes. The StoryMap should label species and case definitions for each event, and should never merge counts from different reports without reconciling their dates and definitions. [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ebola-disease)

**2026 response context:** The [Africa Center for Strategic Studies explainer](https://africacenter.org/spotlight/ebola-drc/) (updated 6 November 2026) discusses insecurity, access, and misinformation in eastern DRC. It is linked from the final StoryMap stop as dated interpretation. Its map and AFP-credited photographs are [not included in the reusable visual catalog](MAP_CATALOG.md#linked-2026-map-without-verified-reuse-rights); later case counts here use the [WHO report of 20 November 2026](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON630).

## Source and attribution policy

- **Outbreak chronology and counts:** Start with the [CDC history](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/outbreaks/) and the event-specific WHO reports in [REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md). Each chart or map should show its source, report date, case definition, and last-updated date. The 2026 outbreak remains in progress; the [WHO report dated 20 November 2026](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON630) is the current reference used here, not a final total.
- **Cross-check index:** Wikipedia's [List of Ebola outbreaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=List_of_Ebola_outbreaks&oldid=1385469746) is a useful, dated checklist for smaller events and links to its cited reports. It is community edited and its 13 November 2026 revision predates the WHO report used here. Verify each selected event and count with the original CDC, WHO, or field-investigation source before mapping it.
- **Historic spatial data:** [Mylne et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2014.42) documented 22 outbreaks and 117 geographic transmission clusters. Cite both the [article](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2014.42) and the [associated data collection](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.1168886) if its records are used; check each downloaded dataset item's license before redistribution.
- **Map basemaps and locators:** The live tour uses [OpenStreetMap tiles](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) with visible “© OpenStreetMap contributors” and [ODbL attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright/attribution-guide). Tour pins are approximate orientation points based on the cited event sources, not case coordinates or a downloadable epidemiological dataset. [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/) is public-domain vector and raster data for future maps.
- **Historical map images:** The [map catalog](MAP_CATALOG.md) lists file-specific public-domain and Creative Commons maps, their creators, underlying outbreak-data sources when identified, snapshot dates, and required credit. License and source information must travel with any reused image or derivative.
- **Photos and video:** External links do not grant republication rights. Check rights for each individual photo or video before embedding, downloading, cropping, or editing it. The [CDC Museum timeline](https://cdcmuseum.org/exhibits/show/ebola/item/7) is linked for context; no image from that exhibit has been copied here.
- **The Lancet perspective:** Its text and portraits are copyrighted by Elsevier. This repository links to and summarizes the lectures; it does not reproduce their pages or images.
- **Splash image:** Original AI-generated conceptual artwork made for this repository in November 2026. It contains no outbreak dataset or accurate geographic locations; the dots and curves are decorative.

The [references](REFERENCES.md) include the publications, source pages, map files, license pages, and media cited here. This is an educational research project; maps and counts should be checked against current public-health reports before use in time-sensitive decisions.
