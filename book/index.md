# Fifty years of Ebola history

**1976–2026 · a geographic and historical guide**

[Open the interactive StoryMap](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026-StoryMaps/) · [Open JupyterLite Lab](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026-StoryMaps/lab/lab/index.html) · [View the source repository](https://github.com/jltobias/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026-StoryMaps)

Ebola disease history begins with **two separate 1976 investigations**, in present-day South Sudan and the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). Across the next 50 years, outbreaks recurred in different places and involved different viruses. The largest epidemic spread through West Africa in 2013–2016. The 2026 Bundibugyo virus outbreak remains an open chapter in this book's **24 November 2026** edition. [Original Sudan investigation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2395561/) · [Original Zaire investigation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2395567/) · [WHO, 20 November 2026](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON630)

The book follows the [15-stop map tour](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026-StoryMaps/) through discovery, recurring emergence, West Africa, eastern DRC, and the outbreaks of 2022–2026. Each chapter links the reports behind its claims. The notebooks use the same tour metadata to make a timeline and approximate geographic locator. **A tour stop is an editorial selection, not an individual case, outbreak count, or complete registry.**

## How evidence is handled

The [CDC outbreak history](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/outbreaks/) and outbreak-specific WHO reports provide chronology; original field studies support the earliest events. Historical maps in the [map catalog](https://github.com/jltobias/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026-StoryMaps/blob/main/MAP_CATALOG.md) have identified creators and reuse terms. The [full bibliography and attribution ledger](https://github.com/jltobias/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026-StoryMaps/blob/main/REFERENCES.md) includes publications, data, media, and map sources. The 2026 figures here come from the [WHO report dated 20 November 2026](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/2026-DON630), with data as of 17 November; they are provisional.

The book is a curated educational synthesis. It does not provide clinical advice or real-time surveillance. Map pins are approximate orientation points for named places or regions. The notebooks make **no estimate of transmission** from those pins.
