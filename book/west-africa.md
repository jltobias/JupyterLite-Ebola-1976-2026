# West Africa, 2013–2016

The West Africa epidemic began in Guinea in late 2013 and became a regional emergency affecting Guinea, Liberia, Sierra Leone, and connected locations. [WHO's retrospective overview](https://www.who.int/emergencies/situations/ebola-outbreak-2014-2016-west-africa) anchors the epidemic's time span and scale. A country-level marker alone cannot convey the epidemic's movement across districts, borders, and cities.

[Baize and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1404505) identified Ebola virus in the early Guinea outbreak investigation. [Gire and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1259657) traced early viral relationships using sequences from Sierra Leone. [Urbanowicz and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2016.10.013) later tested viral glycoprotein variants in cell-based experiments. That laboratory result cannot establish a particular transmission route or rate on the map.

The outbreak also changed research and response. The [Guinea ring-vaccination trial](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)32621-6) helped establish evidence for an Ebola-virus vaccine. Its findings concern disease caused by **Ebola virus**; they must not be silently transferred to Sudan or Bundibugyo virus. [WHO's vaccine guidance](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/ebola-vaccines) distinguishes these species.

## A dated map, not a timeless map

The [CDC situation map of 17 December 2014](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2014_West_Africa_Ebola_virus_outbreak_situation_map.jpg) is a public-domain archival snapshot. It captures what was reported on that date. The [Commons file page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2014_West_Africa_Ebola_virus_outbreak_situation_map.jpg) identifies CDC as creator and records the image's public-domain status; credit CDC and preserve the date if using it. Do not put its 2014 geography beside final epidemic totals without explaining the different reporting moments.

Spatial models can ask what factors helped explain regional spread. [Rainisch and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.3201/eid2103.141845) and [Kramer and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.160294) are useful further reading. A modeled route or risk surface is an inference, not a record of each person's movement.
