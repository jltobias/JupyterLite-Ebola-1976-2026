# Recurring emergence, 1977–2012

Ebola history between the first investigations and West Africa is a series of **distinct outbreaks**, with long gaps and changing geographic evidence. The [CDC chronology](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/outbreaks/) records events in DRC, Gabon, the Republic of the Congo, Uganda, and other locations. [Mylne and colleagues' geographic database](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2014.42) brings together 22 outbreaks and 117 geographic transmission clusters from the historical record; its [associated collection](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.1168886) is a starting point for GIS analysis, subject to each data item's license.

## Three turning points

- **1995, Kikwit, DRC.** [Khan and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1086/514306) document this outbreak, which became a landmark for clinical response, infection prevention, and field investigation. The outbreak year is 1995, despite a conflicting year in one 2026 anniversary lecture.
- **2000–2001, Uganda.** [Lamunu and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijid.2003.04.001) describe the response to a Sudan virus epidemic. A map of this event should identify Sudan virus, rather than assume every Ebola disease outbreak was caused by Ebola virus.
- **2007–2008, western Uganda.** [Towner and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1000212) identified Bundibugyo virus during the outbreak; [Wamala and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.3201/eid1607.091525) describe its epidemiology. The 2026 Bundibugyo outbreak belongs to the same virus-species discussion, but is a separate event.

## Mapping a sparse historical record

Some sources name a settlement; others report a district, health zone, or broader cluster. The notebook's approximate points simplify these differences to orient the reader. A research-quality spatial dataset needs each event's source, time interval, geographic unit, coordinate precision, case definition, and original map or table. The [map catalog](https://github.com/jltobias/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026/blob/main/MAP_CATALOG.md) lists historical maps that may be reused with their required credits.
