# Eastern DRC and overlapping outbreaks

Outbreaks in one country can overlap without being one event. DRC's 2018 Équateur outbreak, the 2018–2020 eastern DRC outbreak, and the separate 2020 Équateur outbreak need different event identifiers and time intervals. The [CDC chronology](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/outbreaks/) distinguishes them. The [2018–2020 response review by Shears and Garavan](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infpip.2020.100038) discusses outbreak control in a setting of conflict and community mistrust.

## Compare snapshots

The European Commission's Emergency Response Coordination Centre (ERCC) published [7 June 2018](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:20180607_DRC_Congo_Ebola_DailyMap.pdf), [31 October 2018](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:20181031_Map_DRC_NordKivu_Ebola_DailyMap.pdf), [22 May 2019](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:20190522_DailyMap_DRC_NordKivu_Ebola2.pdf), and [7 August 2020](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ECDM_20200807_DRC_Ebola_update.pdf) map snapshots. Their Commons file pages state CC BY 4.0. Each is tied to a date and a different geographic situation. Retain the printed data credits and distinguish map-production dates from outbreak dates.

The [PALM therapeutic trial](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1910993) was conducted during the eastern DRC response and changed evidence for treating disease caused by Ebola virus. It should sit on the research timeline beside the outbreak, with its own publication date and trial population. Its results do not establish the same effectiveness for Bundibugyo virus.

The [StoryMap](https://jltobias.github.io/JupyterLite-Ebola-1976-2026-StoryMaps/) uses separate stops and approximate locator pins. The notebook's source audit helps readers check which map and report support each stop.
